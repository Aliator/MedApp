# MedApp: staff calendars and appointments

Status: **built on `feature/staff-calendars`** · 2026-11-16 · **personal templates, meetings, the team view and suggested meeting times built on `feature/team-calendar`** (decisions 50 on) · 2026-11-16

Staff get a **calendar**: an 08:00 to 18:00 working day, UK time, Monday to Friday, with weekends shown greyed as non-working. On it they **book appointments** with patients and **block off time** for activities from a preset list (Lunch, Training, ...). **Day and week templates** fill each week with its usual blocks automatically, and can be changed for one day. Stage definitions say whether a stage **involves a visit** (none, suggested or required); the app then asks for an appointment before the stage can be finished. Appointments go through **Requested** (needs booking), **Booked**, then **Attended** or **Did not attend**, or **Cancelled**.

The owner was away and asked for every choice to be made for them. This document records each one, with the reason, so they can be reviewed and changed. It replaces the to-do item "Appointments and booking".

## 1. Decisions

### The working day
1. **08:00 to 18:00, UK time (Europe/London), Monday to Friday.** Weekends are shown, greyed, as non-working: nothing can be booked or blocked on them. The grid is in 15-minute rows, or 5-minute ones by choice (decision 68). `WorkingHours` (`MedApp.Application/Calendar/Common`) holds the rules.
2. **Everything on a calendar sits inside the working day.** An appointment or a block must start and end on the same weekday, between 08:00 and 18:00 UK time, on a 5-minute boundary, and start before it ends. The server refuses anything else (400). Bank holidays aren't known yet (section 8).
3. **Times are stored and sent as UTC, with a `Z`** (the existing `UtcDateTimeConverter`). Template times are UK clock times (`TimeOnly`), turned into UTC for each date, so a 12:00 lunch is at 12:00 UK time on both sides of a clock change. The calendar response gives each day's `workStartUtc` and `workEndUtc`, so the browser steps through 15-minute slots in UTC without doing time zone arithmetic. A UK working day never spans a clock change (they happen at 01:00 and 02:00).

### What's on a calendar
4. **Activity types** (`CalendarActivityType`): the preset list for blocked time. Admin-managed; each has a name and a colour from a fixed palette (`red`, `orange`, `yellow`, `green`, `teal`, `cyan`, `blue`, `purple`, `pink`, `gray`: Astryx `Token` colours, so contrast comes from the theme). Seeded: Lunch (green), Admin time (blue), Training (purple), Meeting (teal), Annual leave (orange), Travel (gray). Archived, not deleted, when in use (by a block or a template block); archived ones can't be chosen for new blocks, but existing blocks keep them.
5. **Blocks** (`CalendarBlock`): a member of staff, an activity type, a start and end, and an optional note (200 characters). Blocks are about staff, not patients: the note must not hold patient details (the UI says so).
6. **No overlaps.** A new or moved appointment or block may not overlap any appointment (Booked, Attended or Did not attend) or block (stored or from a template) on that person's calendar: the server refuses it with a 409 naming what it clashes with ("That time overlaps Lunch, 12:00 to 13:00."), and the UI only offers free slots. Two requests racing for the same slot are serialised by a database lock per member of staff (`ICalendarLock`, SQL Server `sp_getapplock`), so one wins and the other gets the 409. That lock is a person's, not an appointment's; decision 46 covers two people changing the same appointment.
   - Something that was already on the calendar can come to overlap a later template change (an admin moves everyone's lunch over a booked appointment). Both still show, the appointment on top, and the block is flagged as overlapping; nothing is moved or removed by the app.
7. **Who changes what:**
   - **Any staff member** can see anyone's calendar, and book, reschedule, cancel and record the outcome of any appointment, for any member of staff (as they can work on any procedure).
   - **Blocks, a day's template and the default week** of a calendar: its owner, or an admin.
   - **Activity types:** admins (staff can read them).
   - **Day templates and week templates** are each person's own (decision 50): their owner or an admin changes them; anyone can read them. *(Changed: they were admins' only.)*
   - **Meetings:** admins arrange, change and cancel them; everyone sees them (decision 53).
8. **Past days don't change.** Blocks and templates on a day before today (UK) can't be added, edited or removed (409), and the templates they came from are versioned (decision 11), so a past day keeps showing what it was. Appointments in the past can still have their outcome recorded.

### Templates, applied automatically
9. **A day template** (`DayTemplate`) is named, with blocks given as activity type, start time, end time and an optional note. **A week template** (`WeekTemplate`) is named, with a day template (or none) for each weekday, Monday to Friday.
10. **Each member of staff may have a default week template**, one of their own (decision 50), set by themselves (on the Calendar page, or their Calendar templates page) or by an admin (the same places, for anyone). *(Changed: an admin set anyone's from the admin Calendar templates page, from the organisation's templates.)*
11. **Applied when the calendar is read, never generated.** The calendar works out each day's template blocks when it's shown (`CalendarProjection`), so editing a template changes every day it applies to from then on. Nothing runs in the background.
    - **History is kept by dating rows, not by copying them.** A day template's blocks, a week template's days and a person's default week each have `ValidFrom` and `ValidTo` (UK dates). Editing one closes the rows that changed the day before today and adds new ones from today; rows that didn't change are kept as they are. A row added today and changed again today is just replaced. So a past day projects exactly what applied then, and a change shows from today.
    - **Why today and not tomorrow:** someone editing a template expects to see the result on today's calendar. Today's blocks that have already passed can change with it; that's accepted.
12. **Changing one day's template block** stores an exception for that date (`CalendarBlockException`), so the template block isn't projected that day, and, for an edit, a stored block with the new values. **Removing** it stores only the exception. A later edit to the template keeps exceptions on blocks it didn't change.
13. **"Apply a day template to this day"** stores a day override (`CalendarDayOverride`): that date uses the chosen day template (or none) instead of the week template's. It can be removed again, back to the week's. Blocks added by hand that day stay.
14. **Templates in use are archived, not deleted,** like stage definitions. A day template is in use once any week template or day override has referred to it; a week template once anyone has had it as their default. Archived ones can't be chosen again, but keep applying where they already do (an archived week template stays someone's default until it's changed). Since decision 50 this is per person: their owner (or an admin) archives, restores and deletes them, and "in use" can only mean by the owner's own week templates, overrides and default week.

### Appointments
15. **An appointment** (`Appointment`) is for a patient, optionally for one of their procedures and one of its stages, with one member of staff, a start and end (UTC), a status, optional notes (2000 characters, free text about the patient, treated like procedure notes), who requested or booked it and when, and the outcome's who and when.
16. **Statuses** (`AppointmentStatus`, stored as numbers, never renumbered): `Requested = 0`, `Booked = 1`, `Attended = 2`, `DidNotAttend = 3`, `Cancelled = 4`.
    - **Requested** ("Needs booking") has no member of staff or time yet. **Booked** has all three.
    - **Requested → Booked:** a member of staff, a start and an end, in the future, clash-free.
    - **Booked → Booked:** rescheduling (another time, or another member of staff), in the future, clash-free.
    - **Booked → Attended or Did not attend:** only once its start time has passed. **Attended ↔ Did not attend** can be corrected the same way.
    - **Requested or Booked → Cancelled,** with a reason (500 characters).
    - Cancelled is final. A patient who didn't attend gets a new appointment (the UI offers "Book another").
17. **Booking can't be in the past**, so walk-ins aren't recorded after the event (section 8).
18. **One open appointment per stage.** A stage can have one Requested or Booked appointment at a time; asking for another is a 409 that points to the one there is (when and with whom it's booked, or that it's in Needs booking). Appointments without a stage have no limit. It's enforced in the database too (decision 47).
19. **Every change to an appointment is recorded** in its history (`AppointmentEvent`): requested, booked, rescheduled (with the old time and member of staff), attended, did not attend, cancelled (with the reason) and notes changed (old and new text). Like the procedure audit log, it has no foreign keys, so it outlives what it's about. It feeds the patient timeline (decision 27).
20. **Notifications** (through `INotificationSender`, new kinds, never renumbered): `AppointmentBooked = 1` (an appointment was booked with you, or moved to you), `AppointmentRescheduled = 2` (yours was moved to another time), `AppointmentCancelled = 3` (yours was cancelled, or moved to someone else). Only when someone else did it. Their title is a fixed phrase ("Appointment booked", "Appointment moved", "Appointment cancelled", following main's rule for notification titles, so anonymisation leaves it alone); the message says who and when, never the patient's name or notes. A notification links to the appointment (`Notification.AppointmentId`), opening it on the Calendar page.
21. **Deleting** a patient who has appointments is refused (409), as with procedures. Deleting a procedure (only once it's cancelled or archived) detaches its appointments (procedure and stage set to none) in the same save; their history keeps the procedure. Deleting a user account with upcoming Booked appointments is refused until they're moved or cancelled, so no appointment is left with nobody; so is taking every role from it (decision 48).

### Stage visits
22. **Stage definitions get `VisitRequirement`**: `None = 0` (the default), `Suggested = 1`, `Required = 2`, set by admins when adding or editing a definition. It's copied onto each procedure stage at creation, like the name and instructions. Existing definitions and stages are `None`, so existing templates and procedures behave exactly as before.
23. **The Requested appointment:** when a stage with a visit becomes the current stage (the procedure is created, or the stage before it is finished), `ProcedureChange` creates a Requested appointment linked to it, unless it already has one that's Requested, Booked or Attended (one booked ahead counts). It's recorded in the appointment's history as requested by the app. It isn't made while the procedure is blocked or closed.
24. **Required:** finishing the stage needs an Attended appointment linked to it, checked on the server (`ProcedureWorkflow.WhyStageNeedsVisit`). Staff are refused (409): "The 'X' stage needs a visit before it can be finished: book it, and mark it Attended once it's happened. An admin can finish it without one."
    - **Admin bypass:** an admin may finish it anyway, after a confirmation dialog that asks for a reason (`FinishProcedureStageRequest { withoutVisit: true, reason }`). The stage's `StageFinished` audit entry records `withoutVisit`, the requirement and the reason.
25. **Suggested:** finishing without an Attended appointment asks for confirmation in the UI, for anyone. The server doesn't refuse; the `StageFinished` entry records `withoutVisit` and the requirement.
26. **Tidying up:** finishing a stage cancels its Requested appointments (it doesn't need booking any more), and cancelling a procedure cancels all its Requested ones, each automatically, with a reason saying why. Booked appointments are never cancelled by the app: someone may need to tell the patient, so the procedure page lists them.
    - Finishing a stage again after an admin unlocked it doesn't ask for a visit again.

### Where it shows
27. **The patient timeline** gets an **Appointments** kind: requested, booked, rescheduled, attended, did not attend, cancelled and notes changed, merged into the feed by time. The visibility rules are the timeline's own: staff see the structured values (status, time, member of staff, stage) but **never free text** (no notes, no cancellation reason), and nothing about appointments of a deleted procedure; admins see everything. `TimelineStaffCanaryTests` covers the new entries, and a stage finished without a visit shows that (and, for admins, the reason).
28. **Calendar page** (`/calendar`, side nav, for everyone): day and week views, your own by default, with a staff picker for anyone's.
    - Click an empty slot, or drag across slots, to book an appointment or block time there; the keyboard does the same (arrow keys move between slots, Shift with an arrow extends, Enter opens). A **New** button does it without the grid at all.
    - Click an appointment or block to see and change it in a dialog; drag one to move it, or move it from its dialog.
    - Colours: appointments by status, blocks by activity type, from theme tokens.
    - Its toolbar: Previous, Today, Next, a date picker, Day or Week, whose calendar, and **My default week** (your own default week template). Beside New, **Apply Template** puts a day template (or none, or back to the week's) on days of the week shown (decision 69). *(Changed: it was a **This day's template** menu, in the day view only.)*
29. **Needs booking** (`/calendar/needs-booking`, side nav): a list of Requested appointments, soonest procedure due date first, each with Book.
30. **Booking** is one dialog (`BookAppointmentDialog`), opened from the patient page, the procedure page, a stage that needs a visit ("Book visit"), the Needs booking list, an empty calendar slot, or an appointment (to reschedule): choose the patient (and procedure and stage) unless they're known, the member of staff (the calendar's owner, else the procedure's only assignee, else you), the length (15, 30, 45, 60, 90 or 120 minutes; 30 by default), the day (the week's working days with how many slots are free), then a free slot. Notes are optional. Without a time it can **Add to Needs booking** (a Requested appointment) instead.
31. **Calendar templates** are each person's own page (decision 52): **My calendar templates** (`/calendar/templates`), reached from the Calendar page, or "*name*'s calendar templates" for an admin who picked someone: their day templates and week templates (add, edit, archive, restore, delete when unused) and their default week. *(Changed: it was `/admin/calendar-templates`, admins only, with every member of staff's default week.)* **Activity types** (`/admin/activity-types`, admins): add, edit, archive, restore and delete when unused, organisation-wide as before.
32. **Home** shows your **day calendar**: today, with Previous and Next day, your appointments and blocks. Clicking an appointment opens its procedure (or the patient, when it has none). A customisable widget home screen stays on the to-do list.
33. **Narrow windows and half views:** the week view becomes the day view, the toolbar wraps, and dialogs fill the width.

### Details settled while building
34. **Applying a day template over booked appointments is allowed.** Like a template change (decision 6), it never moves or removes anything: the overlapping blocks show, flagged, and the person deals with the appointments. Only adding or moving a single block or appointment is refused for a clash.
35. **A past Booked appointment can still be cancelled** (for example, it was arranged and never took place) instead of being given an outcome. Rescheduling it moves it to a future time.
36. **Blocking a procedure leaves its Requested appointments alone;** they still need booking when it's unblocked. Only cancelling it cancels them.
37. **Needs booking, by due date,** puts appointments not for a procedure after those that are; the default "start" sort puts Requested ones (no start) last, by when they were requested.
38. **The patient timeline for staff** hides an appointment's entries recorded while it belonged to a procedure that has since been deleted (the entry keeps that procedure's ID). Entries recorded after it was detached have no procedure, so staff see them. Filtering the timeline by procedure leaves out appointments not for a procedure.
39. **Deleting an account with upcoming Booked appointments** is a 400 with the code `HasUpcomingAppointments`, in the Auth slice's existing `IdentityResult` style. Past Booked appointments without an outcome don't block it; they show "Deleted user".
40. **Moving an appointment to someone else** notifies the new person with `AppointmentBooked` and the old one with `AppointmentCancelled`. Booking an appointment straight away records only Booked in its history (not Requested then Booked); booking one to exactly where it already is changes nothing.
41. **Finishing a Suggested stage without a visit** records `withoutVisit` (and a reason, if one was given); only a Required stage needs a reason, and only an admin can give it.
42. **Notification kinds 1 to 9** are for calendar and appointment kinds; 10 and up are reserved for the admin monitoring branch.
43. **Calendar blocks are kept for 12 months after their day** (proposed in `docs/governance/retention-schedule.md`, for the data protection officer to confirm); nothing deletes them yet.
44. **Privacy (merged from #68):** a Restricted patient's appointments can't be booked, rescheduled, given an outcome or have their notes changed, but **can be cancelled**, as their procedures can still be paused (blocked or cancelled); an anonymised patient's can't be changed at all. Anonymising is refused while the patient has a Requested or Booked appointment. Reading appointments is recorded in the activity log (`PatientViewed` for one, `PatientListed` for a calendar or a search), and the subject access export includes appointments and their history.
45. **Appointment notification titles are fixed phrases** ("Appointment booked", "Appointment moved", "Appointment cancelled"), following main's rule for notification titles, so `Notification.HasFixedTitle` covers them and anonymisation leaves them alone (decision 20 updated).
46. **Two people changing one appointment at once** (from the review of PR #93): the calendar lock is per member of staff, so two requests booking one Needs booking appointment with different people didn't wait for each other, and the later save overwrote the earlier; with the same person, the second quietly became a reschedule. Now an appointment has a row version, so the later of two overlapping saves is a 409 ("Someone else changed this appointment just now. Reload it and try again."), for every change, not just booking. And booking says what it expects (`expectedStatus`: `Requested` to book, `Booked` to move): if the appointment isn't in that status any more, it's a 409 ("Someone else has already booked this appointment…"), so a stale "book" can't move someone else's booking.
47. **One open appointment per stage is enforced in the database** by a filtered unique index (`StageId` where the status is Requested or Booked), as well as checked, so two requests at once can't both add one. The one that loses gets the same 409 as the check. A procedure change whose automatic visit loses the race is a 409 to try again, which then finds the visit there is.
48. **Taking every role from someone with upcoming Booked appointments is refused,** with the same `HasUpcomingAppointments` error as deleting them: calendars are only for staff, so their appointments would be on a calendar nobody can open. Keeping either role is fine.
49. **Restricting a record with open appointments asks for confirmation** even with no procedure under way, and the dialog counts both (decision 44: they freeze, but for cancelling).

### Personal templates, meetings, the team view and suggested meeting times (`feature/team-calendar`)
50. **Day and week templates are personal.** `DayTemplate` and `WeekTemplate` have `OwnerUserId` (a member of staff; no foreign key, like other staff IDs), and names are unique per owner, not across the organisation. A person's week template uses only their own day templates, their day overrides only their own day templates, and their default week is one of their own: anything else is a 404, as if it weren't there ("isn't one of this person's templates"), in the slice's existing style for a missing template. The owner adds, edits, archives, restores and deletes (when unused) their own templates and sets their default week; **admins** can do the same for anyone, for support, as they can for blocks (decision 7); **other staff** read anyone's templates (the calendar names the template a day came from) but get a 403 changing them ("Only an admin can change someone else's calendar templates."). Responses carry `ownerUserId` and `canEdit`. The API: `GET`/`POST api/calendar/staff/{userId}/day-templates` and `.../week-templates`; one template's get, edit, archive, restore and delete stay at `api/calendar/day-templates/{id}` and `api/calendar/week-templates/{id}`, open to staff, checked in the handlers. The organisation-wide lists (`GET api/calendar/day-templates`, `week-templates`) are gone. Listing someone who isn't staff (no account, or no role) is a 404; reading one template by ID isn't checked against its owner's roles.
51. **Existing data: a migration that loses nothing** (`MadeCalendarTemplatesPersonal`). Everyone who uses a template, through any dated default-week row (`StaffWeekTemplate`) or a day override, gets their own copy of every template they use, with all its dated rows (`ValidFrom` and `ValidTo` kept): one copy per person per original, shared by their week templates and overrides. A week template's days point at its owner's copies of the day templates, and each person's default week rows, overrides and block exceptions (template block to template block, old ID to new) point at theirs. **The first owner of a template (by user ID) keeps the original, IDs and all**, so the common case (a template one person uses) changes nothing but its owner; the others get copies. Templates nobody uses go to the longest-standing admin (the earliest account created with the Admin role); with no admin they're deleted, since they affect no calendar. So every past and future day of everyone's calendar projects exactly as before: `TemplateOwnershipMigrationTests` projects two people's calendars before and after across past days, a clock change, an override and exceptions, and it was run up and down on a LocalDB database with seeded rows. **`Down`** makes every template organisation-wide again, so calendars still project the same, but can't merge copies back (nothing records which original a copy came from), so a template two people used stays two, each later one renamed "Name (username)" to keep names unique; unused templates deleted on the way up don't come back.
52. **The Calendar templates page moves** from Administration to each person: `/calendar/templates` (and `/calendar/templates/day/...`, `/week/...`), with `?staff=<id>` for someone else's; `/admin/calendar-templates` redirects there. *(The UI's details are in decisions 61 on.)*
53. **Meetings** (`Meeting`, `MeetingAttendee`): something an admin arranges for several members of staff, with a title (100 characters), optional notes (500, about staff, never patients: the UI says so, as for block notes), a start and an end, an organiser and attendees (each once; at least one). The working day's rules apply, as for blocks: one weekday, 08:00 to 18:00 UK time, on 5-minute boundaries (400), and **not on a past day** (409), checked by day as blocks are, so a meeting earlier today can still be recorded; the suggested times and the team view only offer time after now. The organiser is an attendee only if listed (the dialog lists them by default, with a checkbox to take them off). Admins add, change (time, title, notes, attendees) and cancel them (`[Authorize(Roles = "Admin")]`, as for other admin-only actions); every member of staff reads them, on the calendars they can see and at `GET api/calendar/meetings/{id}`. A meeting on a past day can't be changed or cancelled (`canEdit` is false). Cancelling deletes it.
54. **A meeting is busy time** on each attendee's calendar. Booking an appointment or adding a block over it is refused like any other clash, and `CalendarClashes` names it ("That time overlaps the meeting 'MDT', 09:00 to 10:00."). A new or moved meeting may not overlap any attendee's appointments, blocks (stored or from a template) or other meetings: the 409 names who and what ("k.ashford has Lunch, 12:00 to 13:00.", the first attendee by username with a clash). Adding or changing one takes the calendar lock (`ICalendarLock`) of every attendee, in a fixed order, and on a change those of everyone going before as well; if the attendees changed between reading the meeting and taking the locks, it's a 409 ("Someone else changed this meeting just now."), and the meeting's row version catches any other overlapping save the same way.
55. **Meeting notifications** (new kinds in the calendar range, never renumbered): `MeetingInvited = 4`, `MeetingChanged = 5`, `MeetingCancelled = 6`, with fixed titles "Meeting invitation", "Meeting moved", "Meeting cancelled" (so `Notification.HasFixedTitle` covers them), a message saying who, what and when ("admin invited you to 'MDT', Tuesday 17 November, 09:00 to 10:00."), and a link to the Calendar on the meeting's day (`Notification.CalendarDate`). Never for one's own change. Adding someone sends them `MeetingInvited`, removing them `MeetingCancelled` ("took you off"), and moving it `MeetingChanged` to those still going; **a change of title or notes alone tells nobody**.
56. **Deleting an account, or taking every staff role from it,** takes it off every meeting that hasn't started yet (no patient is affected, so neither is refused); a meeting left with nobody is deleted. Past meetings keep their attendees. Nobody is notified (the person is leaving; the others' meeting hasn't changed for them).
57. **Retention and privacy:** meetings are staff data, kept 12 months after their day like blocks (proposed, `docs/governance/retention-schedule.md`), listed in `data-inventory.md` and assessed in the DPIA's staff-data risk 25. Templates are now staff data too (a person's usual working pattern).
58. **The team view's API** (admins): `GET api/calendar/team?date&staffUserIds` returns each person's projected day (the same `CalendarResponse` as one person's calendar, by username), the day itself, and `everyoneFree`: when everyone shown is free, worked out on the server. No `staffUserIds` means everyone with a staff role. Reading it records `PatientListed` for each patient with an appointment on it, like one person's calendar. The calendars are loaded together (`ICalendarRepository.GetCalendarsAsync`: one set of queries for everyone, not one per person).
59. **Suggested meeting times** (admins): `GET api/calendar/availability?staffUserIds&from&to&durationMinutes&exceptMeetingId`, at most 31 days and 100 people; a length that's a multiple of 5 minutes, at most 10 hours. The logic is `Availability` (`MedApp.Application/Calendar/Common`), plain code with its own tests. **Free time:** each working day, the intervals inside working hours and after now when nobody chosen is busy, rounded inwards to 5-minute boundaries. **Busy time** is exactly what a clash check sees (`CalendarClashes.BusyTimes`): Booked, Attended and Did not attend appointments, stored and template blocks (with day overrides and exceptions), and meetings, less the meeting being moved (`exceptMeetingId`). **Suggestions:** up to 10 start times when everyone is free for the whole length, earliest first, on 15-minute steps of the UK clock, at most 3 a day; *also*, within a day, each suggestion starts no earlier than the one before ends, so the three are different options rather than 09:00, 09:15 and 09:30. The response also lists each person's busy times (by username, with what it is, as a clash names it; an appointment is only "another appointment", never a patient), so the dialog can say who's busy at a time chosen by hand. It records nothing in the activity log (no patient data).
60. **No clinical decision:** meetings and suggested times are administrative scheduling of staff time (`docs/clinical-safety/intended-purpose-and-device-decision.md`, version 0.7). No new hazard: a meeting can never be put over a booked appointment (refused as a clash), and nothing moves an appointment for a meeting.

#### In the app
61. **Calendar templates** (`/calendar/templates`, for everyone): tabs **Day templates**, **Week templates** and **Default week** (that person's default, from their own active week templates, replacing the admin page's list of everyone's). Titled "My calendar templates", or "*name*'s calendar templates" for someone else's (`?staff=<id>`). Admins choose whose with `StaffPicker`, the same picker the Calendar page uses. Others can open someone's (a link from that person's calendar is shown only to admins) and see them read-only: no add, edit, archive, restore, delete or default-week change, and the editors open read-only saying who can change them. The week template editor offers only the owner's day templates. `/admin/calendar-templates` (the list, keeping its tab; the old `tab=staff` becomes `default`) redirects there; the old editors' addresses aren't redirected. The side nav's Administration loses Calendar Templates; Activity Types stays. The week list's "used by N people" column became "Default week: Yes/No", since only its owner can have it.
62. **The team view** (the Calendar page's third view, **Team**, admins only; `?view=team`, and anyone else gets the week): one day, a column per member of staff in name order (appointments, blocks and meetings, read as one person's day), after an **Everyone free** column showing `everyoneFree` as the only time that can be selected there. **Who** (a multi-select, everyone by default) is kept in the URL as `who`. Selecting free time in Everyone free offers **New meeting** with those people and that time; in a person's column, **Book appointment** or **Block time** for them (or straight to booking, if only that's allowed). Items open and move as on a person's calendar, but can't be dragged into another person's column. The staff picker, Apply Template, the default week and the templates link are hidden there. In a narrow window or half view it scrolls sideways (decision 67 for the day and week there).
63. **`CalendarGrid` takes columns** (`{key, label?, date, day, items, freeTime?}`) instead of days, so a column can be a person on one date; one person's day or week is `dayColumns(days, items)` and reads exactly as before. A labelled column is named for screen readers "k.ashford, Tuesday 17 November" ("Everyone free, Tuesday 17 November"). Columns keep a minimum width (6rem a day, 8rem a person) and scroll sideways inside the card, with the hour gutter sticky. Selections and moves say their column. **PR #96's behaviour is in it** (a pointer selection reported just after the press ends, the held selection a button, the Book or Block menu's focus handling), in the same code, so the two merge simply apart from the restructured lines.
64. **Meetings on a calendar** are their own kind of item: the title, "N attending", its time, in the token colour of the seeded "Meeting" activity type (`teal`, a constant: activity types can be renamed or recoloured, and a meeting isn't one), with the meeting icon, so it never relies on colour alone. Clicking one opens `MeetingDialog`; admins can drag one to move it (keeping its title, notes and attendees; a 409 shows in the page's message, and it stays put). The booking dialog's free slots and the block dialog's default times count meetings as busy. **Home** shows meetings read-only, like blocks.
65. **`MeetingDialog`**: details for everyone (title, time, organiser, attendees, with "Deleted user" for a deleted account, notes), and for admins **Change** and **Cancel Meeting** (confirmed). The form: title; **Who** (a multi-select with Everyone and Clear; on a new meeting the organiser goes unless they untick "I'm going too", and from the team view they go only if they're one of the people shown); **length** (15 to 120 minutes, 60 by default, or the meeting's or selection's own length); **notes** ("Don't put patient details here"); **suggested times** for today and the next 13 days, as a group of toggle buttons ("Tue 17 Nov, 10:00 to 11:00") with **Later suggestions** (from the day after the last one, or after the range when there were none); or a **day and start by hand**, which says who's busy then from the availability response ("Not everyone is free then: k.ashford has Lunch, 10:00 to 11:00") but still lets them save: the server's 409 is the final word, shown in the dialog, which stays open. Ctrl+S saves, the save and a cancel are announced, and it's one column in a narrow window or half view.
66. **Meeting notifications** in the bell link to the Calendar on the meeting's day (`/calendar?date=…&view=day`), with the meeting icon.

#### Narrow windows (`feature/calendar-narrow`)
67. **The week works in a narrow window or half view, in a compact form.** Before, a narrow window (`useIsNarrow()`: under 1400 px, or a half view) turned the week into a day and dropped Week from the View switch. Now Week is offered at every width: a narrow window still *opens* on a day when the URL names no view, but `?view=week` is kept (the URL parser keeps `week` now, and choosing Week there writes it), so a week chosen there stays one. The **compact week** is that week squeezed in (`CalendarGrid`'s `isCompact`, and `dayColumns(..., {weekdaysOnly: true})`): **Monday to Friday only**, because nothing can be on a weekend (appointments, blocks and meetings are refused there by `WorkingHours.WhyOutside`, and `CalendarProjection` puts no template blocks on one; a weekend column that somehow had something on it would still show, so nothing is hidden); short headings ("Mon 16", each naming its full date to screen readers: "Mon 16: show Monday 16 November"); a narrower hour gutter (2.5rem, not 3) and columns down to 2.5rem; and **every item on one line**, cut short, in its colour, its words in full in a tooltip and its accessible name. Selecting, the Book or Block menu, opening and dragging items, Previous and Next week, the date picker and today's tint work as in the full week. At 620 px of card a day is about 110 px wide; at a phone's 390 px window about 55 px, with no sideways scrolling. Like the day switch before it, it applies after mount (`useIsNarrow()` is false on the server), so the first render matches the server's. The trade-off: a window just under 1400 px wide gets the one-line items too, where the full week would have had room for more lines. The team view is unchanged.
    - **The Book or Block menu is as wide as its buttons** (`width="max-content"` on its `Popover`). Left at its default, Astryx stretches an anchored surface to its anchor's width, so a selection in a wide day column (a narrow window's day view, or the team view's "Everyone free") made a 550 px menu running past the grid.
68. **A choice of 15 or 5 minute rows.** The Calendar page's toolbar has **Row length** (15 min, the default, or 5 min), kept in the URL as `rows=5` (left out for 15), so it survives a reload and the server renders it the same. 5-minute rows are as tall as 15-minute ones, so the day is three times taller and scrolls inside the grid's card, with the day headings and the hour gutter sticky; items keep their places in minutes, so they're three times taller too, with room for more lines. Between the hours the gutter shows the quarter hours, lighter and smaller, and the lines between rows are fainter at 5 minutes than at quarters. Everything goes a row at a time: selecting by pointer or keyboard, dragging items (which keep their length), and the times the booking and block dialogs offer from that page (`stepMinutes`: 5-minute steps). The server already takes any 5-minute boundary (`WorkingHours`), so nothing changed in the API. Switching keeps the same time at the top of the view; opening shows the time now when today is shown and the working day is under way (an hour before it at the top, or less in a short window), otherwise the start of the working day. It works in the day, week, compact week and team views. The trade-off: a 5-minute week has three times as many free-time buttons (600), and the booking dialog lists three times as many start times; and the choice is in the URL rather than a saved setting, so it doesn't follow someone to a link without it.

#### Applying templates (`feature/calendar-apply-template`)
69. **Apply Template replaces the "This day's template" menu.** That menu was in the toolbar, in the day view only, and changed only the day shown. Now an **Apply Template** button sits in the page header beside Needs Booking and New, in the day, week and compact week views, for whoever can change the calendar (its owner, or an admin), never in the team view (it's about one person's calendar). It opens **Apply Template** (`ApplyTemplateDialog`), which fits a narrow window:
    - **Template:** the person's active day templates, then **No template** (an empty day: an override with none) and **The default week's** (removes the day's override, as "Back to the week's template" did).
    - **Days:** a checkbox for each working day (Monday to Friday) of the week shown, each saying what applies now ("Mon 16: Clinic day", "Changed for this day" under an overridden one). **Past days** are shown but can't be ticked ("Past days can't change"), since the API refuses them (409). Ticked at the start: in the day view, the day shown; in a week, today if it's in that week; otherwise nothing. Any number can be ticked.
    - **Apply** (once a template and a day are chosen) makes one call per day, all at once (`Promise.allSettled`): `PUT .../day-template` for a template or none, and for the default week's, `DELETE .../day-template` only on the days that are overridden (the others already follow the week). There's no batch endpoint, and at most five calls didn't seem worth one. Success closes the dialog and announces it ("Clinic day applied to Mon 16 and Tue 17"). If some fail, the dialog stays open and names each day with the API's reason; the days that worked stay applied and are unticked, and the failed ones stay ticked, to try again.
    - **Data:** the day view reads the rest of its week when the dialog opens (the week view already has it: the same query). The person's day templates are read by the page after mount wherever the button shows, and no longer preloaded on the server, since only the dialog shows them.
    - **The trade-off:** applying to several days isn't one transaction, so a failure can leave some days changed and others not; the dialog says exactly which. Opening it from the day view reads the whole week's calendar, which records `PatientListed` for that week's patients, as showing the week would.

## 3. The rules, and where they live

- `MedApp.Application/Calendar/Common`:
  - `WorkingHours`: the working day, slot length, and `WhyOutside(startUtc, endUtc)` for the validators.

  - `TemplateHistory`: replacing a dated row set from today (decision 11), as plain logic with its own tests.
  - `CalendarProjection`: a staff member's days, template blocks and exceptions, from the rows the repository loads (`CalendarData`).
  - `CalendarClashes`: what a time would overlap (`Find`, `FindFirst`), and what a calendar is busy with (`BusyTimes`), meetings included.
  - `Availability`: free time common to several people, and suggested meeting times (decision 59).
  - `MeetingStaff` (attendees must be staff; everyone free at a time, under their locks), `MeetingNotifications`, `MeetingMapper`, `MeetingValidation`.
  - `CalendarMapper`, `CalendarSetupMapper`.
- `MedApp.Application/Appointments/Common`: `AppointmentWorkflow` (statuses, what's allowed), `AppointmentChange` (applies a change, writes its history), `AppointmentMapper`.
- `MedApp.Application/Procedures/Common`: `ProcedureWorkflow.WhyStageNeedsVisit`, and `ProcedureChange.RequestVisit` and `CancelRequestedVisit`, which collect the appointments and history to save with the procedure change (`IPatientProcedureRepository.SaveAsync(ProcedureChange)`), in one `SaveChangesAsync`.

## 5. Device and safety

- **Not a medical device.** Booking a member of staff's time for a patient is administrative scheduling, which the MHRA's guidance names as unlikely to be a device "if it only has an administrative function". The visit rule asks for an appointment because an organisation's own template says a stage involves one; MedApp doesn't decide whether a patient needs a visit, when, or with whom, and computes nothing from clinical data. Recorded in `docs/clinical-safety/intended-purpose-and-device-decision.md`.
- **New hazards,** drafted for the clinical safety officer in `docs/clinical-safety/hazard-log.md`: a missed appointment (a Requested one never booked, or a Did not attend not followed up), a double booking, a bypassed required visit, a booking at the wrong time (time zones and clock changes), the wrong patient booked, and appointments left with a deleted account.

## 6. Privacy

Appointment notes and cancellation reasons are free text about patients, and block notes free text about staff. They're listed in `docs/governance/data-inventory.md` and assessed in `docs/governance/dpia.md`, with retention in `retention-schedule.md`.

**With the privacy features (PR #68):** anonymising a patient replaces their appointments' notes and cancellation reasons, and the notes, reasons and procedure titles in their appointment history, with placeholders; notifications about their appointments keep their fixed titles (`Notification.HasFixedTitle`, decision 45). Anonymising is refused while the patient has a Requested or Booked appointment. The subject access export includes their appointments and appointment history. A restricted patient's appointments can't be booked, rescheduled, given an outcome or have their notes changed (the restriction guard's 409), but can still be cancelled with a reason, so care can be paused safely, as their procedures can; an anonymised patient's can't be changed at all. Reading appointments (the calendar, Needs Booking, the booking dialog, an appointment) is recorded in the activity log as reads of the patients they show, as other reads are.

## 8. Left out, for later

- Bank holidays and other closed days, and working hours per person or per site.

- Overdue Requested appointments (requested long ago and still not booked) flagged or notified.
