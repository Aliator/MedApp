**How to use it.** Most customers already have a privacy notice for patients. If you do, add the MedApp section (section A) to it, and check the rest against section B. If you don't, adapt the whole template. Choose one option where there are alternatives, delete the guidance in italics, and fill in the brackets. Your DPO must approve the result.

Write it so your patients can understand it: plain words, short sentences, and a version for children if you treat them. UK GDPR Articles 13 and 14 list what it must contain.

## Section A: wording about MedApp

> **The systems we use to coordinate your care**
>
> We use a system called MedApp to record your details, to record the referral that asked us to see you (with its letter), to organise the steps of pathways you have with us, such as [pre-assessment, the pathway itself and follow-up], and to book your appointments with our staff. Our staff record notes at each step and about appointments, and may keep copies of documents about your care in it, such as letters, test results, scans and photos. The system keeps a record of who made each change, and who opened each document, and when, including whether you attended an appointment and why one was cancelled.
>
> MedApp is provided by [Company name], which runs it for us. [Company name] acts only on our instructions. It can't use your information for its own purposes, and it's bound by a contract and a duty of confidentiality.
>
> Your information is stored in the UK. [Company name] and its hosting provider, Microsoft, can reach it only to run and support the system, under contracts that bind them to our instructions and to confidentiality. [Company name]'s support staff look at records only when we ask them to, under a written policy. In rare cases Microsoft's support engineers may do so from outside the UK, under the safeguards UK law requires.
>
> In our organisation, our staff who have a MedApp account can see your record. [We record who looks at your record, and we check these records regularly.] *MedApp records access to records (roadmap step 7). Include the sentence in brackets once your organisation checks those records regularly.*

## Section B: a full notice

> **How [Customer name] uses your information**
>
> **Who we are.** [Customer name], [address], is responsible for your information (the "controller"). Our data protection officer is [DPO name], [DPO contact]. [Our Caldicott Guardian is [name].]
>
> **What we hold about you.** Your name, date of birth and email address, and your NHS number [if you record it]; the pathways you are having with us, and their dates and progress; your appointments with our staff (when, with whom, and whether you attended, or why one was cancelled); the referral that asked us to see you (who referred you, when, to which of our services, how soon they asked for you to be seen and why), with a copy of the referral letter, and how long you have waited since, including any time we paused the wait because you chose to wait or weren't available, and why; notes our staff write about your care; and copies of documents about your care, such as letters, test results, scans and photos [of the area being treated]. [Add anything else you hold in other systems.]
>
> **Where it comes from.** From you, and from [the professional who referred you, your GP, other services caring for you]. When you're referred to us, the referral letter comes from the professional who referred you (usually your GP practice); our staff record its details in MedApp, including the name of the professional and their practice, and keep a copy of the letter. [*Only once MedApp is connected to it:* We may check your name and date of birth against the NHS's national record of patients' details (the Personal Demographics Service), using your NHS number.]
>
> **Why we use it.** To provide and coordinate your care, to book your appointments, to keep an accurate record of it, and to make sure the right staff do the right things at the right time.
>
> **Our legal basis.** *Choose one:*
> - *NHS bodies:* We use your information to carry out our public task of providing health care (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e)). Because it's health information, we also rely on Article 9(2)(h), for the provision of health care, and the Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2.
> - *Private providers with a contract with the patient:* We use your information to carry out our contract with you to provide your care (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(b)). Because it's health information, we also rely on Article 9(2)(h), for the provision of health care, and the Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2.
> - *Private providers without a contract with the patient (for example insurer-funded care):* We use your information because we have a legitimate interest in providing and recording your care (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f)). Because it's health information, we also rely on Article 9(2)(h) and the Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2.
>
> Everyone who sees your information is a health professional or has a duty to keep it confidential.
>
> **Who we share it with.** Our staff caring for you. Our suppliers who run our systems for us, including [Company name] (MedApp) and its hosting provider, Microsoft, who can reach it only to run and support the system, under contracts that only let them use it for us. [Others: your GP, other services caring for you, your insurer, as you'd expect.] We don't sell your information, and we don't use it for marketing.
>
> **Where it's kept.** Stored in the United Kingdom. In rare cases Microsoft's support engineers may reach it from outside the UK to support the system, under the safeguards UK law requires.
>
> **How long we keep it.** We keep health records for the periods in the NHS Records Management Code of Practice: usually 8 years after your treatment ends, and for children until their 25th birthday (or 26th if they were 17 when treatment ended). [Other periods for mental health, maternity or cancer care, if they apply.] After that, we review the record and securely destroy it if it's no longer needed. Copies in our backups are deleted within [12 months] after that.
>
> **Automated decisions.** We don't make decisions about you by automated means alone.
>
> **Your rights.** You have the right to:
> - ask for a copy of your information
> - ask us to correct information that's wrong (if you disagree with a clinical opinion, we can record that you disagree)
> - ask us to restrict how we use it while a concern is looked at
> - object to how we use it [*NHS bodies and legitimate interests only*]
> - ask us to move it to another provider [*contract basis only*]
> - ask us to delete it, though we usually need to keep health records for the periods above
>
> To use these rights, contact [contact]. We'll reply within one month. It's usually free.
>
> **The National Data Opt-out.** *NHS-funded care in England:* Your information in MedApp is used for your care, so the National Data Opt-out doesn't apply to it. If we ever used it for research or planning, we would apply your opt-out choice. You can set your choice at [nhs.uk/your-nhs-data-matters](https://www.nhs.uk/your-nhs-data-matters/).
>
> **Complaints.** If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, contact our data protection officer at [DPO contact]. We'll acknowledge your complaint within [5 working days] and reply as soon as we can. You can also complain to the Information Commissioner's Office: [ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint](https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/).
>

## Notes for customers

- **Children.** If you treat children, give a short version they can understand, and explain how a parent or guardian can ask for their information.
- **Accessibility.** Publish the notice in an accessible format, and offer it in other formats and languages on request.
- **Where to publish it.** On your website, in waiting areas, in appointment letters, and in the information given before a pathway.
- **Keep it true.** If you change how you use MedApp (for example sharing records with another organisation), update the notice before you do it.
- **Referrals.** If you accept referrals, say who they come from in "Where it comes from" (the patient didn't give you this information, so Article 14 applies). A referral names the referring clinician; that's their personal data too, which your notice for professionals, or your contracts with referrers, should cover.
- **Appointment reminders.** MedApp doesn't send patients reminders or confirmations. If you send them from another system, your notice should say so.
