This is the **product DPIA**: the supplier's assessment of MedApp as a hosted service. It follows the structure of the ICO's DPIA template ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/2553993/dpia-template.docx)). Each customer is a controller and must carry out **its own DPIA** before using MedApp. It can adopt this one as the starting point, then add its own context: its patients, its staff, how it will use MedApp, and its own controls.

## Summary

- **A DPIA is mandatory.** MedApp is designed for processing health data, a special category, and across customers it's large scale (Article 35(3)(b)).
- **Today's high risks:** any staff member can see any patient (reads are now recorded, and admins can see who opened a record, but nothing restricts or alerts on it); two-factor sign-in is optional, so an account without it still signs in with a password alone; any staff member can still delete a patient with no pathways (now recorded in the activity log); nothing is ever deleted on a schedule, and deleted files' contents are kept indefinitely; there's no monitoring to notice a breach; backups have never been restored; on Azure the app would use the database administrator's login; and **uploaded files aren't scanned for malware** before staff open them (risk 22).
- **Specimens and lab reports** (risk 29) apply since PR 13 (branch `feature/specimens`): medium before its measures, low with them.
- **Planned:** risks 35 to 40, for the community specialist centre's Phases 3 to 5 (consent and questionnaires, insurers, data-request packs, several organisations, incoming referrals, video consultations), are drafted from the design in [Step 5](#planned-risks-the-community-specialist-centres-phases-3-to-5). None applies until its feature's PR merges, which completes it. **Built so far:** risk 29 (specimens and lab reports, PR 13), risk 30 (letters, PR 16), risk 31 (contact details, PR 12), risk 32 (texts and emails to patients, PR 26), risk 33 (scheduled jobs, PR 19) and risk 34 (MDT lists, PR 30).
- **Planned for the multi-clinic centre:** risks 41 to 48 (`docs/multi-clinic-plan.md`): clinic separation, what centre-wide search reveals, the lab's lasting access, admins' read-all access, hospital patients, reports to the hospital, consult and MDT access, and clinical photos. Two are drafted high (41, a missed team filter; 48, clinical photos). None applies until its MC PR merges.
- **Conclusion: MedApp must not hold real patient data until the measures marked "before go-live" in section 6 are in place.** That's roadmap steps 2, 4, 5 and 7, **plus the patient banner from step 6** and **malware scanning of uploaded files, brought forward from step 9** (or a deployment run without file storage, which turns uploads off), which together make it ready for a first pilot. The banner is in the gate because risk 8 (notes or files put on the wrong patient's record) stays high until it's built; the rest of step 6 isn't needed for the gate. **The banner is now built** (branch `feature/nhs-integrations`), and counts for the gate once the Clinical Safety Officer has verified it. Malware scanning is in the gate because risk 22 (a malicious file opened on a clinical workstation) stays high without it. With these measures, every risk in this assessment is reduced to low or medium, so **prior consultation with the ICO (Article 36) isn't expected to be needed.** If any high residual risk remains at go-live, including risk 8 if the banner isn't built or risk 22 if files are accepted unscanned, the processing must not start without consulting the ICO.

## Step 1: The need for a DPIA

**What MedApp does.** MedApp is a web application for clinical and administrative staff. It records patients, and tracks the pathways carried out for them through a fixed sequence of stages, each with clinical notes. Staff can attach files (scans, photos, results, letters) to a patient, a pathway or a stage. Every change to a pathway, and every file added, deleted or downloaded, is recorded in an audit trail. Each member of staff has a calendar, on which staff book appointments with patients and block off time (lunch, training, annual leave). Staff register **referrals** received from other clinicians (usually GPs), with the letter, and they wait in an inbox until a clinician of the specialty triages them (triage comes with a later release). Staff record **specimens** taken at a stage, print a label and request sheet, and send them to a lab; lab staff record them received and attach the lab's report as a file. Admins manage staff accounts, pathway templates, calendar templates and assignments. The supplier, [Company name], runs it as a hosted service on Microsoft Azure in the UK, for customers such as clinics, private hospitals and NHS trusts.

**Why a DPIA is needed:**
- **Article 35(3)(b):** processing on a large scale of special category data (health). The supplier processes it for many customers.
- **The ICO's list** of processing likely to result in a high risk includes processing of health data, and new technology.
- **NHS buyers** ask for a product DPIA through DTAC's data protection section ([DTAC form 2.0](https://digital.nhs.uk/binaries/content/assets/website-assets/services/dtac/dtac_form_2.0_february_2026.docx)).

## Step 2: The processing

### Nature

- **Collection:** the customer's staff type patients' details and clinical notes into MedApp in a browser, and upload files they already hold (a scanned referral letter, a photo of a wound, a result from another system). **Referrals** are a second source: a referrer (usually a GP practice) sends a letter, and staff type its details (the referring practice and clinician, the priority asked for, the reason for referral) into MedApp and attach the letter; nothing from the referrer reaches MedApp directly. There's no import or patient-facing access. A demographics lookup can fill a patient's name and date of birth from their NHS number, but only an offline stub of synthetic people exists; the real Personal Demographics Service (PDS) isn't connected, and connecting it would make NHS England a source of patient data and need this DPIA updated first.
- **Storage:** in Azure SQL (UK South), reached only through a private network endpoint, with point-in-time backups. Attached files are in Azure Blob Storage in the same region, also reached only through a private endpoint, with 7 days' soft delete and no other backup; their details (name, type, size, who uploaded them) are in Azure SQL.
- **Use:** staff find patients, start pathways from templates, work stages in order, write notes, and see their task lists. Staff also see each patient's timeline on the patient page: who changed what on their pathways and when, with old and new target dates, priorities and statuses, past assignees, and the usernames recorded at the time (deleted accounts' included): structured values, never free text (no notes, titles or reasons from the audit trail), and only for pathways that still exist. Admins assign work, unlock finished stages, read the full audit log, see a patient's whole history on screen (the same timeline with its free text, deleted pathways included), and manage accounts. Admins can also set **monitoring rules** (Administration, Monitoring): to be told in the bell when a particular staff member, or anyone, does something to a pathway (creates one, finishes a stage, completes, blocks or cancels one, is assigned, unlocks a stage, changes a target date), optionally only for a priority, template or stage. Each person chooses whether they're told when they're assigned a pathway. Staff upload, list and download any patient's files; listing and downloading are recorded in the activity log, and every download in the pathway audit log too. The timeline shows that a file was attached or deleted, with its name for admins only. Files are only ever downloaded, never shown in the page; the only images shown are small thumbnails the server makes from PNG and JPEG photos.
- **Calendars and appointments:** staff book a patient with a member of staff at a time, optionally for a pathway's stage, with optional notes. An appointment goes from requested to booked, then attended or did not attend, or is cancelled with a reason; every change, including old and new notes and the reason, is kept in its history. When a stage that involves a visit becomes current, MedApp requests an appointment for it. Staff block off their own time for activities from an admin-managed list (Lunch, Training, Annual leave, ...), with an optional short note; each person's own day and week templates fill their week with its usual blocks. Admins arrange **meetings** for several members of staff (a title, optional notes about staff, a time and the attendees); a meeting is busy time on each attendee's calendar, and every member of staff can see it. Admins also see a **team view** (several people's day side by side) and **suggested meeting times** (when everyone chosen is free), computed from the same calendars; neither names a patient. The patient timeline shows appointment history: to staff, structured values only (status, time, member of staff, stage), never notes or cancellation reasons; to admins, everything. **After a missed appointment** (PR 26), booking staff see it on Requires Appointment's **Did not attend** view until someone records a follow-up (rebook, clinician to review, or no further appointment, with a detail), kept in the appointment's history; staff see the decision on the timeline, admins the detail too. MedApp never discharges, re-prioritises or changes a pathway because of a missed appointment.
- **Referrals:** staff with the `RegisterReferrals` permission register a referral for a patient (adding the patient first if they're new), edit it while it awaits triage, or close it as withdrawn or entered in error, with a detail; every change is in the referral's history, with structured values only (never its free text). Referrals awaiting triage wait in an inbox, oldest first, with their count in the side nav. Staff with `ViewPatients` read referrals, their free text included, as they read notes; opening one is recorded in the activity log, and referral lists record the patients they show. The patient timeline shows a referral's history: to staff, structured values only (the specialty, the priority asked for, the status, which fields were edited); to admins, also the reason, the referring practice and the detail given when it was closed. Clinicians triage referrals (accept into a pathway, reject or redirect), recorded on the referral. Each referral has a **waiting-time clock**: whole days from when it arrived against the centre's own target for its specialty and priority (a clinician sets the priority), shown as "Day 84 of 126" and "Breached"; staff who work on pathways pause it for a delay the patient chose or caused, with a reason, an optional detail and a review date, which becomes the pathway's target date. It's date arithmetic on dates staff entered, never a clinical judgement or ranking, and a breach is shown, never sent to anyone.
- **Specimens:** staff with `RecordSpecimens` record a specimen taken at a stage (its type, lab, requesting clinician, and optionally the site and request notes for the lab), print its label and request sheet, and record it sent; lab staff with `ProcessSpecimens` record it received, rejected or reported, attaching the lab's report as a file; either marks it lost. Each has MedApp's own number, printed on the label as text and as a QR code holding only the number. The Lab page lists them by where they are (to send, expected, in the lab, overdue, rejected and lost, reported), and finding one by its number opens it. Every change is in the pathway audit, with structured values only. Staff with `ViewPatients` read specimens, their free text included; opening one, its label or request sheet is recorded in the activity log. The patient timeline shows staff the number, type, lab and status, never the site, notes or any detail; admins see those too. Results sign-off (a clinician signing off each report) comes with the next release, updating this DPIA.
- **Sharing:** none outside the customer electronically, except emails to staff (password reset links, notices of changes to their own sign-in, and, for those who choose them, short-lived two-factor codes), sent through Azure Communication Services Email; they carry a staff member's username and email address and never patient data (a code email carries only the code). **And, when the customer turns them on, texts and emails to patients** about their own appointments (PR 26; risk 32): reminders, and a message when staff move or cancel an appointment and ask for the patient to be told, or (if turned on) the day after a missed one, only on the channels the patient chose. They go through **GOV.UK Notify**, the customer's own processor, only in the Live mode, which is off by default and configured nowhere in the repository or chart. Each holds the patient's mobile or email and fixed wording (the organisation, the date, time and site), never why. Within the customer, every staff user can see every patient, and every staff member's calendar and every appointment (section 5, risks 1 and 25). A read-only FHIR API returns a patient's details, by ID or NHS number, in the NHS's standard format, but only to a caller signed in as one of the customer's staff, and each use is logged (risks 1 and 21); there are no system-to-system clients yet. **Specimens go to an external lab** (risk 29): the specimen with its printed label (the patient's name, date of birth and NHS number) and request sheet (adding the site, the request notes and the requesting clinician) travels by hand or courier, outside MedApp, under the customer's own agreement with the lab; the report comes back by the lab's own route and is uploaded as a file.
- **Deletion:** staff can delete a patient who has no pathways, no appointments and no files. Admins can delete cancelled or archived pathways that have no files; their audit entries remain, and their appointments are kept, detached from the pathway. Admins can anonymise a patient's record in place (identifying details and free text replaced, appointments' notes and cancellation reasons included, the structure kept); anonymising also renames the patient's files and deletes them, with an audit entry for each. Staff can delete files they uploaded, and admins any file, except on a restricted or anonymised record, where nobody can add or delete files. A deleted file is hidden from staff and can't be downloaded, but its details and its contents are kept until a purge job removes the contents after a retention window. **No purge job exists yet**, so deleted files, anonymised patients' included, are kept indefinitely. Nothing is deleted on a schedule: records past their retention period are listed for an admin to review.
- **New technology:** server-side rendering (a Node page server: in Kubernetes a container of its own in the API's pod, without the API's secrets; run on its own, inside the API's container), live notifications over WebSockets with a Valkey relay, Kubernetes, and cloud blob storage for files, with thumbnails made on the server (SkiaSharp). A scheduled job sends patients' reminders without a person (risk 33), but decides nothing about them: it sends what the patient chose, about appointments people booked. None involves profiling, AI or automated decisions: no file is read, classified or interpreted. Monitoring rules tell an admin about single actions as they happen; they don't score, rank or judge staff, and decide nothing (risk 24).
- **Sources and flows:** [data-inventory.md](data-inventory.md).

### Scope

- **Data:** name, date of birth, email and (optionally) NHS number of patients; pathway titles, notes (several per pathway, each with every version kept), target dates, priorities and statuses; stage notes (clinical free text); **attached files** (scans, photos, results, letters, up to 20 MB each) and their names (free text); the audit trail with copies of notes and file names, and a record of each download; appointments (patient, member of staff, time, status, notes and cancellation reasons) and their history with copies of the notes and reasons; referrals (the specialty asked for, the date received, the referring practice, clinician and ODS code, the priority asked for, the reason for referral, the status, who registered and closed it, and the detail given) with their history (structured values only) and letters; specimens (MedApp's number, the type, the lab, the requesting clinician, the site and request notes, who took, sent, received and reported each and when, the lab's and courier's references, a rejection reason and closing detail) with their history (structured values only) and the lab's report as a file; staff usernames, password hashes, roles, the specialties each belongs to, optional work email addresses, two-factor keys and recovery codes, sessions with IP addresses and browsers, settings with saved searches; each member of staff's own notepad (free text, which may hold patient details; the whole notepad is cleared only once it has gone 30 days without any change, so what's in it stays while it's used; risk 26); staff calendars (blocked time with its activity, such as annual leave or training, and a short note); notifications, and each person's notification choices; admins' monitoring rules, which name the staff they watch; each patient's reminder choice, every text and email sent to them (or that couldn't be: the destination and text as sent, its status and who asked) and a missed appointment's follow-up (a decision and a detail). SNOMED CT codes on stage definitions label kinds of step and aren't personal data. Field by field: [data-inventory.md](data-inventory.md).
- **Special category:** health data about patients, including that a patient is being seen and whether they attended. Free text can also hold other special category data (for example religion, sexual life, ethnicity) if staff write it. **Files** are health data by nature, can be **images of the body**, keep the **metadata** they were uploaded with (for a photo, possibly where and when it was taken), and can hold **third parties'** data (a letter naming relatives or other clinicians). **A referral names its referrer:** the referring clinician is a third party's personal data, received from the referrer, not the patient. MedApp is not meant to hold health data about staff, but a calendar block's note could ("hospital appointment"; risk 25).
- **Volume:** per customer, [patients a year], [staff users]. Across customers, large scale.
- **Retention:** proposed in [retention-schedule.md](retention-schedule.md); **not enforced today**.
- **Geography:** patients in the UK; data stored in the UK.

### Context

- **Relationship:** patients are receiving care from the customer, and expect their information to be recorded and kept confidential. They don't use MedApp themselves. **Staff** are the customer's employees (or contractors): there's an imbalance of power, so monitoring them must be necessary, proportionate and open (risk 24).
- **Control:** patients have no direct control over what is recorded; they rely on the customer and its staff.
- **Vulnerable people:** patients include people who are ill, children, and possibly people lacking capacity.
- **Expectations:** patients would expect a care record system, including copies of their letters and results; they wouldn't expect staff outside their care to browse their record or their photos, or their data to be used for anything else.
- **Prior concerns:** the ICO wrote to over 200 health leaders on 12 November 2026 about staff "snooping" on records, after more than 300 reports of unauthorised access in 2026 ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/11/uk-health-sector-warned-to-tackle-medical-record-snooping/)). NHS suppliers have been fined for weak security, including missing MFA ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2025/03/software-provider-fined-3m-following-2022-ransomware-attack/)).
- **State of the technology:** MedApp is young (built from January 2026), has one maintainer, and has never run in production ([architecture handbook, 1.5](../architecture.md#15-status-and-maturity)). The code is in a public repository.
- **Codes and standards:** the NHS Records Management Code of Practice, the Caldicott Principles, the DSPT, DCB0129 and DCB0160, OWASP ASVS, Cyber Essentials.

### Purposes

- **For patients:** their pathways are coordinated safely, in order, by the right staff, with an accurate record, and the documents their care needs (letters, results, images) are with it; the visits their pathways need are booked, and visits that are needed aren't skipped by mistake.
- **For the customer:** coordinating work and staff time, accountability through the audit trail, meeting record-keeping duties, and supervising work as it happens (admins' monitoring rules), for example a team lead keeping an eye on a new member of staff's work. Monitoring is supervision, not clinical follow-up, which doesn't rely on it (hazard H-29).
- **For the supplier:** providing the service under contract. No purpose of its own ([roles-and-lawful-bases.md](roles-and-lawful-bases.md)).

## Step 4: Necessity and proportionality

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Lawful basis** | The customer's: typically 6(1)(e) (NHS) or 6(1)(b)/(f) (private), with 9(2)(h) and DPA 2018 Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2 ([roles-and-lawful-bases.md](roles-and-lawful-bases.md)). The supplier acts on instructions under Article 28. **Texts and emails about a patient's own appointments** are part of their care, under the same bases, and are service messages, not marketing, so PECR's consent rule for marketing doesn't apply [for the DPO to confirm]. The patient's reminder choice is a preference MedApp always respects, not the lawful basis, although the code names a missing choice `NoConsent`. |
| **Does the processing achieve the purpose?** | Yes. Coordinating a pathway's stages needs the patient's identity, the pathway, and notes on each stage. Booking a visit needs the patient, the member of staff and the time, and booking around staff time needs to know when staff are unavailable, but not why. |
| **Is there a less intrusive way?** | Paper or spreadsheets hold the same data with weaker access control and no audit. A system without patient identity can't coordinate care for a person. |
| **Minimisation** | **The patient record is deliberately small:** name, date of birth, email and the NHS number, and, all optional, a postal address, a mobile and the registered GP practice; no sex. **The address** is there because letters (Phase 3) must reach the patient; **the mobile** because reminders (Phase 4) go to it, on the channels the patient chooses; **the practice** because clinic letters and discharge summaries go to the patient's GP, from a directory rather than retyped (risk 31). None is required, and none is used for anything else. **The NHS number is optional,** and justified by safe identification: NHS England mandates it as the patient's identifier in health and care, it's what the patient banner shows to tell patients apart (risk 8), and it's how other NHS systems find the same patient. It's never written to the activity log. Further identity details step 9 adds will each be justified then. **Free text isn't limited**, and the audit copies it (risks 3 and 4). **Files aren't limited in content** either, only in type (six kinds, found from their contents) and size (20 MB); their names are free text, and originals keep their metadata (risk 23). Appointments hold only what booking needs; their notes are optional and limited to 2,000 characters, cancellation reasons to 500, and calendar block notes to 200. Blocked time records an activity from a fixed list, not a reason. Appointment notifications carry no patient name or notes. **Messages to patients** say only who they're from, when and where, never why (`PatientMessageTexts`), and go only on the channels the patient chose; a missed-appointment message is off unless the customer turns it on. |
| **Accuracy** | Staff can edit patients and pathways. An NHS number must pass its check digit, and can't be on two patients; it isn't traced against PDS, so it isn't confirmed to be the patient's. Finished stages lock, and only an admin can unlock them, which is audited. Patient edits are recorded in the activity log (which fields changed, not their values), and an admin can mark a record as disputed by the patient. A file put on the wrong record can't be moved, only deleted (soft, audited) and uploaded again to the right one (risk 8). |
| **Retention** | Periods in [retention-schedule.md](retention-schedule.md), based on the Records Management Code; attached files are part of the clinical record. Clinical records past their period are listed for review (keep with a reason, or anonymise); operational data isn't purged yet, and deleted files are kept until a purge job exists (risk 5). |
| **Transparency** | Template privacy notices for customers to give patients and staff ([privacy-notice-patients-template.md](privacy-notice-patients-template.md), [privacy-notice-staff-template.md](privacy-notice-staff-template.md)). |
| **Rights** | Supported by the product: a subject access export, restriction and dispute flags, anonymisation; the clinician review of an export is by hand ([data-subject-rights.md](data-subject-rights.md)). The export lists a patient's attached files, but doesn't include the files themselves yet. |
| **Processors** | The supplier, under an Article 28 agreement ([dpa-template.md](dpa-template.md)); Microsoft as a sub-processor ([sub-processors.md](sub-processors.md)), whose Azure Blob Storage holds the files. For texts and emails to patients, in the Live mode only, **GOV.UK Notify, as the customer's own processor** under its own agreement with Notify; the customer's DPIA covers it (risk 32). |
| **International transfers** | None by the supplier; UK hosting ([international-transfers.md](international-transfers.md)). Where GOV.UK Notify and its delivery providers process messages is [to confirm with GOV.UK Notify's documentation], and is the customer's to check before turning the Live mode on. |
| **Monitoring staff** | Admins' monitoring rules process **staff** personal data: what a named member of staff does, as it happens. **Necessary:** supervising clinical and administrative work (supporting a new member of staff, knowing when work is blocked or cancelled) is part of running a service, and the same facts are already in the audit trail admins can read; the rules only bring them sooner. **Proportionate:** only admins can have rules, and only while they're admins; each admin sees only their own; the events are workflow actions already audited (no reads, searches, sign-ins, locations or timings beyond the action's own); a notification carries no free text and no patient identifiers; nobody is told about their own actions; at most 50 rules each. **Open:** staff are told in the staff privacy notice ([privacy-notice-staff-template.md](privacy-notice-staff-template.md)), and the customer should have a monitoring policy that says why and how admins use it, following the ICO's guidance on monitoring workers. Risk 24. |
| **Function creep** | The supplier commits to no use of its own. Any new purpose needs a DPIA update first. |

- **Severity of harm:** minimal, significant or severe.
- **Overall risk:** low, medium or high, from the two together, by this matrix:

| Likelihood | Minimal | Significant | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Possible** | Low | Medium | High |

## Step 6: Measures to reduce the risks

**Status:**
- **In place:** built, in the code or the deployment design. Azure is written but **not yet deployed**, so its controls are "in place in design".
- **Planned:** in the roadmap ([architecture handbook](../architecture.md#roadmap-the-next-10-steps)), with the step.
- **Before go-live:** must be done before MedApp holds real patient data.

**Residual risk** is given twice: **now** (with the measures in place) and **after** the planned measures.

### Risk 2: account takeover

| Measure | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Passwords stored as salted hashes by ASP.NET Core Identity | In place | `MedApp.Infrastructure/Common/Identity` |

| Two-factor compulsory for admins from day one (today an admin turns the requirement on), passkeys; SSO with the customer's identity provider; first admin with a chosen name | Planned, step 4. **Before go-live** | |
| A breached-password check | Planned, step 2. **Before go-live** | |
| Find and reset accounts that may still have `DefaultPassword1` | Planned (`docs/todo.md`). **Before go-live** (no such accounts exist in a new deployment) | |

**Residual risk:** now **high** while two-factor is optional (**medium** once a customer requires it for admins); after steps 2 and 4 **low**.

**Staff email addresses** add a little personal data of staff (a work address, given by them or their admin) and a new recipient (the email service). They're used only to reset a forgotten password and to tell the person about changes to their own sign-in, so a compromised account is noticed. Emails never carry patient data, clinical free text (a notification's title is a pathway's title, so notifications are *not* emailed) or a password, and a welcome email is only a link to confirm the address. Customers should tell staff in their staff privacy notice ([privacy-notice-staff-template.md](privacy-notice-staff-template.md)).

## For customers adapting this DPIA

Add to your copy:
- **Your context:** which services and patients, how many staff, which sites, whether children or people lacking capacity are involved.
- **Your lawful bases** and your privacy notice.
- **Your controls:** joiners, movers and leavers; device security, including anti-malware on computers that open attached files, and clearing downloads on shared computers; training; who your admins are; how you'll review access reports once they exist; what your staff may write in appointment notes and calendar block notes.

- **Your sign-off:** your DPO, and your Caldicott Guardian for NHS and social care.
- **DCB0160:** your clinical safety assessment of deploying MedApp, separate from this DPIA (roadmap step 6).
