**How to use it.** This is the supplier's standard agreement with each customer for the processing of personal data in MedApp. It sits alongside the main service agreement. Text in square brackets is to be filled in or decided for each customer. It contains the terms UK GDPR Article 28(3) requires ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/contracts-and-liabilities-between-controllers-and-processors-multi/what-needs-to-be-included-in-the-contract/)); the clause for each is noted in brackets.

**NHS and public sector customers** often contract on their own terms (for example a G-Cloud call-off contract, or the NHS Standard Contract's data processing schedule). Then use this template as a checklist: make sure their terms cover everything here, and complete their schedule with the details in Schedules 1 to 4.

## 1. Definitions

- **Data Protection Legislation:** UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018, the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003, and any law that amends or replaces them, including the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.
- **Controller, processor, personal data, processing, data subject, personal data breach, special category data:** as defined in UK GDPR.
- **Customer Personal Data:** personal data processed by the Processor on behalf of the Controller under the Main Agreement, described in Schedule 1.
- **Sub-processor:** another processor engaged by the Processor to process Customer Personal Data.
- **Working day:** a day other than a weekend or a public holiday in England.

## 2. Roles and scope

2.1. The Controller is the controller, and the Processor is a processor, of Customer Personal Data. Schedule 1 sets out the subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the types of personal data and the categories of data subjects (Article 28(3)).

2.2. The Controller is responsible for having a lawful basis, and the Article 9 condition, for the processing, for the accuracy of the data it enters, and for giving data subjects privacy information.

## 3. Instructions (Article 28(3)(a))

3.1. The Processor processes Customer Personal Data only on the Controller's documented instructions, including about transfers outside the UK, unless UK law requires otherwise. In that case the Processor tells the Controller before processing, unless that law forbids it on important grounds of public interest.

3.2. The Controller's instructions are: this agreement; the Main Agreement; the Controller's use of MedApp's features by its authorised users; and any further written instruction from a named contact of the Controller in Schedule 1.

3.3. The Processor tells the Controller at once if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes Data Protection Legislation.

3.4. **The Processor does not process Customer Personal Data for any purpose of its own.** In particular it does not use it for analytics about users or patients, profiling, marketing, training or testing artificial intelligence, research, benchmarking, or sale, and does not combine it with other data. Aggregate measurements of the service that contain no personal data (such as uptime and error rates) are not Customer Personal Data.

## 4. Confidentiality (Article 28(3)(b))

4.1. The Processor ensures that everyone it authorises to process Customer Personal Data is bound by a duty of confidentiality, by contract or by law, that continues after their engagement ends, and that it covers the common law duty of confidentiality owed to patients.

4.2. The Processor limits access to the people who need it to provide the service, under its support access policy ([support-access-policy.md](support-access-policy.md)), and gives them data security training at least every 12 months.

## 5. Security (Article 28(3)(c) and Article 32)

5.1. The Processor takes the technical and organisational measures in Schedule 2, which are designed to ensure a level of security appropriate to the risk, given the special category data involved.

5.2. The Processor keeps Schedule 2 accurate. It may change the measures, but not so as to reduce the overall level of security. It tells the Controller of any material change.

5.3. Where Schedule 2 lists a measure as **planned**, the Processor will use reasonable endeavours to put it in place by the date shown, and tells the Controller if a date will slip.

## 6. Personal data breaches (Article 33(2))

6.1. The Processor tells the Controller of a personal data breach affecting Customer Personal Data **without undue delay, and in any case within [48] hours of becoming aware of it**, by email to the Controller's breach contact in Schedule 1, and by phone in working hours. The limit becomes 24 hours from the date the Processor has out-of-hours cover, a planned measure in Schedule 2. The Processor names a deputy for breach notices in Schedule 1.

6.2. The first notice includes what is known at the time. The Processor then provides, as soon as they're known: the nature of the breach, the categories and approximate numbers of data subjects and records concerned, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed, and the name of a contact. It sends updates at least every 24 hours until the breach is contained, or at intervals the Controller agrees.

6.3. The Processor takes reasonable steps to contain the breach and reduce its effects, keeps evidence, and cooperates with the Controller's investigation and any notification to the ICO and to data subjects. The Processor does not notify the ICO or data subjects about a breach of Customer Personal Data on the Controller's behalf, unless the Controller asks it to in writing.

6.4. The Processor's procedure is in [breach-response.md](breach-response.md).

## 7. Sub-processors (Articles 28(2), 28(3)(d) and 28(4))

7.1. The Controller gives general written authorisation for the Processor to engage sub-processors. The sub-processors approved at the date of this agreement are in Schedule 3.

7.2. The Processor gives the Controller **at least 30 days' written notice** before adding or replacing a sub-processor. The Controller may object on reasonable data protection grounds within that time. The parties then discuss it in good faith. If they can't resolve it, the Controller may end the affected service without penalty and with a pro-rata refund of fees paid in advance.

7.3. The Processor engages each sub-processor under a written contract with data protection obligations that are at least as protective as this agreement.

7.4. The Processor remains fully liable to the Controller for each sub-processor's performance of its obligations.

## 8. Transfers outside the UK

8.1. The Processor stores and processes Customer Personal Data **only in the United Kingdom**, and does not transfer it outside the UK, or allow access to it from outside the UK, without the Controller's prior written authorisation, except as clause 8.2 allows.

8.2. **Emergency access by the Processor's own employees.** If an incident needs urgent action to protect the service or the data, an employee of the Processor (not a contractor or sub-processor) who is temporarily outside the UK may access the service to contain or recover from it, using a company-managed device. The access is recorded in the support access log, limited to what the incident needs, and reported to the Controller within 24 hours. The data stays stored in the UK. Because the employee is part of the Processor, this isn't a restricted transfer under UK GDPR Chapter V.

8.3. Schedule 3 records any limited access by a sub-processor from outside the UK that the Controller authorises, and the safeguard that applies (Article 46 or adequacy regulations).

## 9. Data subject rights (Article 28(3)(e))

9.1. The Processor helps the Controller, by appropriate technical and organisational measures, to respond to requests from data subjects to exercise their rights.

9.2. If a data subject contacts the Processor directly, the Processor passes the request to the Controller **within 2 working days**, and does not respond to it except to say it has been passed on, unless the Controller instructs otherwise.

9.3. Until MedApp's self-service export and rights features are available (Schedule 2), the Processor carries out exports, corrections and erasures on the Controller's written instruction **within 5 working days, or as agreed**, under the procedure in [data-subject-rights.md](data-subject-rights.md), [charged as the Main Agreement says].

## 10. Other assistance (Article 28(3)(f))

The Processor helps the Controller, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to it, to meet its obligations under Articles 32 to 36: security, breach notification, communicating breaches to data subjects, DPIAs, and prior consultation with the ICO. This includes providing the product DPIA ([dpia.md](dpia.md)) and keeping it up to date. [Assistance beyond this is charged at the rates in the Main Agreement.]

## 11. Deletion or return at the end (Article 28(3)(g))

11.1. When the Main Agreement ends, the Controller tells the Processor within **30 days** whether it wants Customer Personal Data returned or deleted.

11.2. **Return:** the Processor provides a complete export in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format (JSON, with a readable summary), by a secure transfer method agreed with the Controller, within 30 days of the request.

11.3. **Deletion:** the Processor deletes Customer Personal Data from the live service within **30 days** after the return is confirmed, or after the Controller asks for deletion.

11.3A. **If the Controller doesn't answer** within the period in 11.1, the Processor doesn't delete. Because the data includes health records the Controller must keep for years, it sends at least two reminders, 14 days apart, to every contact in Schedule 1. If there's still no answer, it returns a complete export, as in 11.2, to the last named contact by the agreed secure route, and deletes the live data only after that contact confirms receipt, or [6 months] after the export is sent, whichever is sooner. Copies in backups are deleted as the backups expire, within the period in Schedule 4, and are not restored in the meantime except for disaster recovery, after which the deletion is applied again.

11.4. The Processor confirms deletion in writing.

11.5. This clause doesn't apply to data UK law requires the Processor to keep. The Processor tells the Controller of any such requirement, and keeps that data confidential and only for that purpose.

## 12. Information and audits (Article 28(3)(h))

12.1. The Processor makes available to the Controller the information needed to show it meets Article 28 and this agreement. That includes, as they become available: its DSPT status, its Cyber Essentials certificate, a summary of its most recent penetration test, its ISO/IEC 27001 certificate, and its policies.

12.2. The Processor allows and contributes to audits, including inspections, by the Controller or an auditor it appoints, **once in any 12 months**, or after a personal data breach, or when the ICO requires it. The Controller gives at least 30 days' notice (except after a breach), and the auditor is bound by confidentiality. Audits are done so as not to disrupt the service or expose other customers' data.

## 13. Records and cooperation

13.1. The Processor keeps a record of processing under Article 30(2) ([ropa.md](ropa.md), part A).

13.2. The Processor cooperates with the ICO, on request, in the performance of its tasks (Article 31).

13.3. The Processor has appointed a data protection officer: [DPO name], [DPO contact].

## 14. Liability

[To be agreed, and consistent with the Main Agreement. Note that a processor can be fined by the ICO in its own right, and that each party is liable for damage it causes under Article 82.]

## 15. Term, precedence and law

15.1. This agreement lasts as long as the Processor processes Customer Personal Data, and survives the end of the Main Agreement until clause 11 is complete.

15.2. If this agreement conflicts with the Main Agreement on the processing of personal data, this agreement prevails.

15.3. This agreement is governed by the law of [England and Wales], and the courts of [England and Wales] have exclusive jurisdiction. [For a customer in Scotland or Northern Ireland, consider its own law.]

## Schedule 2: security measures

The measures in place, and those planned with the date they'll be in place. The DPIA ([dpia.md](dpia.md), step 6) links each to the code.

**Before signing,** check each "in place" item in the live environment. As of this draft, MedApp's Azure environment is written and checked in CI but has never been deployed, so the Azure items are in place in the design only.

| Area | In place | Planned (by) |
|---|---|---|
| **Hosting** | Microsoft Azure UK South; managed Kubernetes (AKS) with Entra ID-only access; Azure SQL with public access off and a private endpoint | An Azure Policy allowing only UK regions; private endpoints for Key Vault and the registry; a restricted cluster API ([date]) |
| **Encryption** | TLS for all traffic from users; HSTS; TLS to the database (1.2 minimum); encryption at rest with Microsoft-managed keys | A customer-managed encryption key; encrypted traffic to the message relay ([date]) |
| **Access control** | Individual accounts; salted password hashes; server-generated first passwords; lockout after 5 failures; two roles checked on every request to patient and account data; admin-only user management; the full audit trail is admin-only, and staff see a per-patient timeline without its free text; server-side sessions of 8 hours (extendable on request to 24 at most from sign-in), in a protected cookie; sign-in throttling; the most common passwords refused; sessions ended when a password or roles change, and on request by an admin | MFA, compulsory for admins; single sign-on; an idle timeout ([date]) |
| **Audit** | Every change to a procedure recorded with who, when and what, kept after deletes | Every read and change of patient data, sign-ins, and account changes recorded; access reports; tamper-evident storage ([date]) |
| **Application security** | Output escaping; no raw HTML in notes; one origin; no caching of pages or API responses with personal data; security headers and a Content Security Policy; dependency, code and image scanning on every change; required automated tests and reviews of every change | Closing the gaps in the ASVS Level 2 review; a penetration test ([date]) |
| **Platform security** | Default-deny network policies; the `restricted` Pod Security Standard; non-root, read-only containers; secrets in Key Vault, never in source code; no stored cloud credentials | A passwordless, least-privilege database identity; Defender for Cloud ([date]) |
| **Resilience** | Two copies of the application; point-in-time database restore for 7 days | 35-day restore and geo-redundant backups within the UK; tested restores every quarter; RPO 15 minutes and RTO 4 hours ([date]) |
| **Monitoring** | Built-in telemetry, not yet exported | Central logs in the UK; alerts; out-of-hours cover, which moves the breach notice in clause 6.1 to 24 hours ([date]) |
| **Data management** | Synthetic data only in development and testing | Retention enforcement; a subject access export; archiving and erasure workflows ([date]) |
| **People** | A support access policy (draft, to approve before signing) | Confidentiality clauses in every employee's and contractor's contract; MFA on every company account [to confirm]; annual training; pre-engagement checks; Cyber Essentials; the DSPT ([date]) |
