> **Your MedApp account: how we use your information**
>
> **Who is responsible.** [Customer name] is responsible for the information about you in MedApp. Our data protection officer is [DPO name], [DPO contact]. MedApp is provided and run for us by [Company name], on servers in the United Kingdom, acting only on our instructions.
>
> **What MedApp holds about you:**
> - **Your account:** your username, your roles (staff or admin), the specialties (services, such as Dermatology) our admins say you work in, and your password, stored only in a scrambled form (a "hash") that nobody can read back.
> - **Your sign-ins:** each time you sign in, the time, your device's IP address, and your browser and operating system. MedApp also counts failed sign-ins, and locks an account for 5 minutes after 5 failures.
> - **What you do:** every change you make to a pathway or an appointment is recorded with your name and the time: for example starting or finishing a stage, the notes you write (old and new text), status changes, and booking, moving or cancelling an appointment, recording one after it happened, or withdrawing one recorded in error. **If you ask MedApp to tell the patient** by text or email when you move or cancel an appointment, or cancel a clinic session, the message records that you asked; it's kept with the patient's record and is in their copy of it if they ask for one. **If you record what happens after a patient didn't attend** (rebook, clinician to review, or no further appointment), it's recorded with your name, the time and the detail you give; other staff see the decision on the patient's timeline, and admins the detail too. So is every file you attach, delete or download. **Referrals:** registering a referral, editing it, withdrawing it or marking it entered in error is recorded in the referral's history with your name and the time, and so is the detail you give when you close one; so is every triage decision (accepting a referral into a pathway, rejecting or redirecting it), and the reason you give; so is pausing or resuming a referral's waiting-time clock, with the reason and detail you give; whoever registered the referral is told your username and the outcome. **Specimens:** each specimen records, by your account, whether you were its requesting clinician or recorded it taken, sent, received, reported, rejected, lost or entered in error, with the time and any detail you give; staff and the patient (in their copy of their record) see you by your username. MedApp also records which patient records, timelines, pathways, appointments and specimens (including their labels and request sheets) you open, the patients your patient and pathway lists, calendars, booking lists and the Lab worklist show you and which search fields you used (not what you typed), including through MedApp's FHIR interface, when you look up an NHS number (not the number), the lists of attached files you open, changes to patients' details, and when you sign in and out (and failed attempts), with your computer's network address. The names of the patients you've pinned in the side menu aren't recorded each time they're shown.
> - **Your calendar:** your working hours (the days and times you work, and at which site, kept with the dates they applied from), your clinic sessions (when, in which room and for which specialty you run a clinic, and the clinics that repeat), your appointments with patients, and the time you block off, with its activity (for example lunch, training or annual leave) and any note you add. Your own day and week templates (your usual pattern of blocked time), which week template is your default, and any day changed to another day template. The meetings our admins arrange for you, with their title, notes, time and who else is going, whether you and each of the others accepted or declined (and when), and, if you're an admin, the meetings you arrange. Everyone who can see a meeting sees these replies.
> - **Letters you write, approve or send:** each letter records who wrote it, who it was submitted to, who approved or returned it (with the comment), and who sent it to whom and how, by name and time, kept with the patient's record. Your full name and job title are printed on letters you approve.
> - **Your full name and job title, if you or an admin give them:** as they will appear on letters you write or approve (for example "Dr Sam Patel, Consultant dermatologist"). You set them in Account Settings; our admins can set them too, and see them on the Users page. A change is recorded (which of the two changed, by whom and when, not what it says). They go with your account.
> - **Your email address, if you give one:** used only to confirm it, to send you a link when you forget your password, to tell you when how you sign in changes (your password, your address or two-factor authentication), and, if you choose codes by email, to send you sign-in codes. Emails never contain patient information or passwords.
> - **Two-factor authentication, if you turn it on:** which way you chose (an authenticator app or codes by email), the key your authenticator app shares with MedApp, your one-time recovery codes, and, for codes by email, a scrambled form of each code we email you, kept for minutes.
> - **Your settings:** your display preferences, accessibility settings and saved searches. They're stored with your account so they follow you between computers, and in your browser.
> - **Your notepad:** what you write in it, with when you last changed it. Only you can see it. It's kept with your account, never in your browser, and cleared once you've gone 30 days without changing it at all (while you use it, everything in it stays), or when you clear it or your account is deleted. It's for jottings: anything about a patient belongs in their record (use Add note to pathway), not in the notepad, where it isn't part of their record.
> - **Your notifications:** for example when someone assigns you a pathway (you can turn that one off in Account Settings), books, moves or cancels one of your appointments, records one you had after it happened, closes a day on which you have appointments, blocked time or meetings, withdraws as entered in error the only visit of a finished stage on a pathway you're assigned to (or, if you're an admin, any such visit), or invites you to a meeting, moves it or cancels it, or declines a meeting you arranged (these quote the meeting's title), or, for a specimen you took or are the requesting clinician for, when the lab rejects it or someone marks it lost (these name the specimen's type, never the patient). If you ask for a note to be deleted, every admin is told that you asked (not your reason), and you're told whether it was deleted or kept; your request, with your name and reason, is kept with the patient's record and is in their copy of it if they ask for one. If an admin deletes a note you wrote, you're told (by their username, never the reason), unless you asked for it yourself.
> - **If you're an admin, or your role lets you monitor work (such as a service manager), your monitoring rules:** what you've asked to be told about, and about whom.
> - **Your roles,** which decide what you can do in MedApp (for example Clinician, Nurse or Booking), and who changed them and when. **If you're an admin,** the changes you make to roles themselves (making, renaming, archiving or deleting one, and which permissions you added or removed), with when; the other admins are emailed about each.

## Notes for customers: rules to give staff with the notice

Give these to every member of staff with a MedApp account. They support the DPIA's measures for free text and shared computers ([dpia.md](dpia.md), risks 1, 3 and 10).

- **Your account is yours.** Never share your password or use someone else's account.
- **Give MedApp an email address of your own,** not a team or shared mailbox: anyone who reads the mailbox can reset the password of every account that uses it. Check it's typed correctly; you confirm it yourself, signed in to MedApp.
- **Look only at the records you need** for the care you're giving or the work you're doing.
- **Patients' contact details and appointment messages:** when a patient gives or changes their mobile number or email address, read it back to them, and ask whether they'd like texts or emails about their appointments; record their choice, and change it as soon as they ask (they can't reply to the messages). Tick "Tell the patient" only when the patient should hear of the change by text or email, and still tell them by your usual route when it matters (a texted reminder is a courtesy, not your only contact). When you record a follow-up to a missed appointment, write in the detail who decided and why, nothing more.
- **Registering a referral:** check you have the right patient before you register it, and if MedApp says the patient already has an open referral to that service, check it isn't the same one. Type from the letter only what the referral needs (the reason for referral), not the whole letter; the letter itself is attached. If MedApp refuses a referral because the patient's record is restricted, give the letter to an admin straight away, so it isn't lost.
- **Specimens:** check the label against the patient (name, date of birth and NHS number) before the specimen leaves you, and the lab checks it against the request sheet. Write in the site and request notes only what the lab needs to test it. Attach a report from the specimen's own page, found by its number, and check the patient shown before you do.
- **Write only what's needed** in notes, titles and reasons, including appointment notes and cancellation reasons: no unnecessary details about other people, and no identifying details in pathway titles beyond what's needed. Old notes and reasons are kept in the record's history, so a later edit doesn't remove them.
- **Your calendar is seen by all staff.** When you block off time, choose the activity and leave the note empty or generic ("Away"): never a health or family reason, and never a patient's details.
- **Sign out** when you leave a shared computer, and lock your screen when you step away.
- **Don't save searches by patient name** on a shared computer.
- **Patient searches stay in the browser's history** (the search terms are part of the page address, so lists can be shared as links). On a shared computer, use your own browser profile, or clear the history when you finish. Signing out removes your saved searches and pins from the browser.
- **Don't copy patient information** out of MedApp into email, messages, documents or AI tools.
- **Attaching files:**
  - Check you're on the right patient, and the right pathway or stage, before you attach a file. If you attach one to the wrong place, delete it at once and tell [contact].
  - Attach only what the patient's care needs. Name a file for what it is (for example "pre-op bloods"), not for the patient.
  - Files aren't checked for viruses yet: each shows "Not scanned". Open them only on [the organisation's] computers, with their anti-virus up to date. *Delete this rule once MedApp scans files.*
- **Downloaded files** stay on the computer after you sign out. Open them only on [the organisation's] computers, never save them to a personal device or a shared folder, and delete them when you've finished.
- **Admins: monitor for a reason.** Set monitoring rules about a named member of staff only for a reason your monitoring policy allows (supervising or supporting their work), and delete them when it no longer applies.
- **Report** anything that looks wrong (records you shouldn't see, changes you didn't make) to [contact] at once.
