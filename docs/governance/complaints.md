Since 19 June 2026, a controller must make it easy to complain about how it handles personal data, acknowledge a complaint **within 30 days**, take appropriate steps to respond without undue delay, and tell the complainant the outcome without undue delay (Data Protection Act 2018, section 164A, added by the DUAA) ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/about-the-ico/media-centre/news-and-blogs/2026/06/new-data-protection-complaints-law-now-in-force/)).

## Which complaints this covers

| Complaint about | Who handles it |
|---|---|
| **The supplier's own processing** (customer contacts, marketing, support correspondence, recruitment, its website) | **The supplier**, under this procedure |
| **A patient's or staff member's data in MedApp** | **The customer**, which is the controller. If the complaint reaches the supplier, it's passed to the customer **within 2 working days**, as for rights requests ([data-subject-rights.md](data-subject-rights.md)), and the complainant is told who to contact. The supplier helps the customer investigate. |

## The procedure

| Step | When |
|---|---|
| **Acknowledge** the complaint, with a reference and who is handling it | Within **5 working days** (the law allows 30 days; the supplier aims for faster) |
| **Investigate:** the DPO looks at what happened, with whoever was involved | As soon as possible |
| **Respond** with the outcome, what's been done, and the right to complain to the ICO | Within **30 days** of receipt where possible; if longer, tell the complainant why and when to expect it |
| **Record** the complaint, the steps and the outcome in the complaints log | Every complaint |
| **Learn:** if the complaint shows a wider problem, fix it, and update the DPIA or a policy | After closure |

If the complaint reveals a personal data breach, follow [breach-response.md](breach-response.md) at once.

The complaints log is kept **3 years** after closure ([retention-schedule.md](retention-schedule.md), section 7). The DUAA allows regulations requiring controllers to report the number of complaints they receive; the log makes that possible.

## The ICO

Anyone can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (formally the Information Commission since 30 September 2026): [ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint](https://ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint/). The ICO will normally expect the complainant to have complained to the controller first.
